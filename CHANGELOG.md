## [2026.10.7] - 2026-10-12 — the queue is a calendar, and links follow your accent

### Changed
- **The SN Queue card shows the coming six weeks as a calendar (owner's design, 2026-10-12).** A grid from the Monday of this week, Monday first, in the site's timezone: every scheduled note is a dot inside its day, under the number (all of them, not only the next four; past three on one day, two dots and "+N"), today has a solid outline and an underlined number, the next-up day is tinted and carries a small corner square, empty days are dimmed, and the 1st of a month shows the month over its number and dot. Days inside a gap you marked Planned get a quiet dashed outline, explained in a line under the grid. Tap a day, or move to it with the arrow keys (Home and End go to the week's ends), and a box under the grid names that day's notes with title, time and a link to the editor; it opens on the next-up day and follows it as notes publish, and a day you pick (even an empty one) stays picked through the card's refreshes, so "Next up" is said there now instead of in a separate headline. A note whose schedule passed says "Overdue" in place of its time, and one missed before this week is named over the grid ("1 overdue: <title>"). Notes past the six weeks are counted ("+14 more through Jan 25, 2027"). The drafts are now listed by title too (the newest three you can edit, each linking to its editor; an editor also sees "+N more"; a contributor sees only their own). Still shown: the scheduled count and the date the queue runs to, the draft count, the gap line with its Planned button, Just published and Open Scheduled. Removed: the "Then" list (its notes are dots on the grid and named when you pick their day; one past the six weeks is listed under "Later") and the separate "Next up" headline (its title and time are in the box). Changed: the relative times on those rows ("Tomorrow 1:01 PM", "Fri 2:08 PM", "Oct 20") now read as the day's name over the clock time ("Next up · Tue, Oct 13", "1:01 PM"); "Overdue" stays. Why: the owner asked for a calendar for the notes to come and a list for the ones gone. The card is taller: its default height goes from 380 to 780, sized for the busiest state and measured live at the default 312 width (774 with a missed note, a gap line, three drafts and three Later rows; an ordinary day needs 594; the old card already needed 418 there and scrolled); a card you resized keeps its size, so on your desktop it scrolls until you drag it taller once. Pinned by tests/desktop-status-resilience.cjs (every note in the window is a mark across six weeks including a 1st of the month and a five-note day; the 1st shows tag, number and dot; today is marked on exactly one cell and the next-up day has its corner mark; drafts by title; each day's accessible name carries the date and titles; arrow keys and one tab stop; an untouched card follows the next-up day; a picked empty day and the keyboard focus survive a changed reading and a failure; Overdue; seven columns that cannot overflow at the narrowest card), tests/abilities-content-queue.php (the reading carries every scheduled note with its site-timezone day and time, today, the week's Monday on a Sunday too, planned gaps, the drafts this user can edit and escaped edit links) and tests/desktop-mode-integration.php (the 780 budget).

### Fixed
- **S&N links follow your accent pick in OS Settings › Appearance.** The desktop cards' "Open Analytics →", "Open Provenance →", "Open Health →", "Machine readers in S&N Analytics →" and the rest, and every link in the S&N Home and S&N Analytics windows, were OpenStation's fixed Nebula pink (#ec9bff), a color the accent picker never changes. They now take the picked accent, lifted toward white only as far as AA for link text needs (4.5:1 on every dark window and card surface): your red reads as #ff6767 (6.2:1 in a window, 6.7:1 on a card); Indigo, Emerald and WordPress Blue are lightened the same way; Sirius, Nebula and other light picks are used exactly as picked. In the light windows of the Legacy desktop theme the pick is darkened instead; its desktop cards stay dark and keep the lift. S&N Analytics links, which painted the raw accent (4.4:1 for red, under AA), use the same color. Figure changed: link color, pink #ec9bff to the accent-derived color.
