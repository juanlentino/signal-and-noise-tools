ABOUT

I grew up in Buenos Aires, a city where music bleeds through the walls and every conversation has a rhythm underneath it. Panacea, my recording studio, opened there in 2016. I ran the technical side and led the production team. The sonic identity of everything that came through the door was mine to shape.

Full Sail University came next. I finished the Bachelor of Science in Music Production as Valedictorian, with the Advanced Achiever award. Today I'm completing an MBA in Applied AI in Business at Westcliff University while producing and engineering for artists and labels across the Americas.

Twenty years in, the count sits past 50 collaborations and more console hours than I have bothered to track. Great sound is not purely a technical achievement. Most of it is knowing when to push, and when the silence is already doing the work.

Practice · Studio and Clients

THE WORK CROSSES BORDERS.

Panacea still runs out of Buenos Aires. The freelance practice runs out of Orlando, serving independent artists and labels from Argentina through Colombia, Mexico, and the United States. Cross-market work is not a line on a website for me. It is the daily condition of the job, and it comes with a specific education.

Here is what that education taught me. The systems that carry credit and payment across borders are held together by assumptions, and those assumptions fail quietly. A session musician plays on a record in one country. The record is registered in another. Somewhere between the two, the name comes loose from the work.

The same discipline that runs a session extends into creative business consulting and AI workflow integration for studios, labels, and agencies. Systems, problems, the thing working when it did not work before.

Research · Provenance

WHO MADE THIS, AND HOW DO WE KNOW.

Detection was the industry's first answer to AI-generated music. Train a classifier to spot synthetic output, run it against the catalog, flag what it finds. The classifier lags the models it chases, and the false positives land on legitimate artists. As generation improves, detection degrades. That degradation is permanent, because the thing being detected keeps getting better at not being detected.

Provenance inverts the problem. A cryptographic signature applied at the moment of creation, carried through the distribution chain in a tamper-evident metadata layer, does not have to guess. It verifies or it fails to verify. There is no confidence interval to argue about.

That claim is the spine of two papers on SSRN, Provenance Over Detection and Provenance as Substrate.

The second paper takes the argument from authorship into identifier infrastructure. ISRC, ISWC, and the rest of the identifier stack were designed for a world where registration happened once, on a form, in a room. The unmatched royalty pool at the MLC runs into the hundreds of millions of dollars, which is what identifier failure looks like at scale. Identifiers that are self-issuing, collision-resistant, and signed at creation would let the legacy codes keep working as aliases while the underlying record holds.

Service · The Field

PEER REVIEW, AND THE VOTE.

I am a voting member of The Recording Academy and The Latin Recording Academy, both since September 2025. Membership in either is by invitation, granted on peer review of the professional record. The vote is the Grammy and Latin Grammy vote, which means listening to work in the categories where my judgment carries weight and abstaining in the ones where it does not.

I review for LAMIR, the Latin American Music Information Retrieval workshop.

The craft deserves to be taken seriously at every level. That includes the work no one bills for.

Education & Mentorship

WHAT I KNOW, I PASS ON.

The gap between what audio programs teach and what the studio actually demands is something I think about a lot. Theory gets you in the door. Session management, client communication, knowing a take is the one before the artist does: that part is earned behind the glass. I mentor emerging producers and engineers to close the gap faster than I did.

In practice that means mix critiques with context, rather than "turn up the vocals." Pricing the work. Building a client base from zero. Running a session on the day nothing goes according to plan. None of it fits a curriculum, and all of it separates someone who knows the tools from someone who can deliver under pressure.
